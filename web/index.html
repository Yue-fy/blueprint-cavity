THE PHYSICAL QUESTION

How can light fit into a space this small?

In hexagonal boron nitride, or hBN, infrared light couples to vibrations of the crystal. The resulting waves can be confined inside a cavity far smaller than the wavelength of light in free space.

A mode is a characteristic field pattern. A resonance is a frequency where the cavity responds strongly. The explorer makes these otherwise invisible patterns easier to inspect.

What this case lets you verify.

Cavity brings together stored physics simulations, interactive measurement choices and a mathematical answer checker. Blueprint has its own drawing tasks, saved agent runs and scoring rules. Each case keeps its evidence and evaluation scope visible.

Data and evaluation scope

The 3D explorer displays precomputed fields. The lab documents numerical convergence limits, including field details that are not yet converged. Changing the view does not run a new simulation.

The reply checker tests numeric agreement for a fixed mathematical example; it does not score a proof or verify a cavity design. Recorded mathematical evaluations are available separately and are not physical cavity-optimization runs.
